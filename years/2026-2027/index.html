Yingxin Li

Nankai University

An equivariant Landweber exact functor theorem

Date: November 16, 2026 Time: 4:00 PM, Beijing time
See full abstract

Chromatic homotopy theory studies the periodic phenomena in stable homotopy theory through formal groups. For a compact abelian Lie group \(G\), these ideas admit natural \(G\)-equivariant refinements. In this talk, I will give an overview of \(G\)-equivariant formal groups, and then give an equivariant version of the Landweber exact functor theorem, which provides a criterion for when a \(G\)-formal group law over a graded ring \(R\) can be realized by a \(G\)-equivariant complex oriented cohomology theory. As an application, I will discuss the Landweber exactness of \(G\)-formal groups arising from non-equivariant formal group laws and from elliptic curves.

Nov 02

William Balderrama

University of Bonn

Smooth knots in the metastable range via orthogonal calculus

View abstract

The homotopy groups of spheres are closely tied to classification problems in differential topology. Seminal work of Haefliger gave a homotopy classification of the smooth knot group \(C_n^q = \pi_0 \mathrm{Emb}(S^n,S^{n+q})\) in codimension \(q\geq 3\) in terms of the fiber of the unstable \(J\)-homomorphism. I will describe how this classification may be combined with the methods of Weiss's orthogonal calculus to identify the groups \(C_n^q\) in the metastable range \(n \leq 3(q-2)\) with the homotopy groups of a certain Thom spectrum over \(BO(2)\).

Oct 18

Sven van Nigtevecht

University of Bonn

Synthetic modular forms

View abstract and resources

Topological modular forms play an essential role in modern homotopy theory, in particular in computations of the homotopy groups of spheres. This requires an understanding of the homotopy groups of tmf. In this talk, I will explain a longstanding circularity in the literature on this computation, and a resolution of it through the use of a synthetic spectrum called Smf (synthetic modular forms). The construction of Smf is part of a larger result that lifts spectral sequences to synthetic spectra, allowing for comparisons to Adams--Novikov spectral sequences as well as a host of computational techniques to be applied. This is joint work with Christian Carrick and Jack Davies.
